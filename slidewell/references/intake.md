# Intake

**Covers:** what to ask the user before building: two short rounds of questions with sample answers, and the section menu of building blocks for each deck type.

**Doesn't cover:** what to do with the answers (`storytelling.md` for the story, `visual-identity.md` for the look).

The goal of intake is to learn just enough to write a storyline the user will recognize as right. Ask in **two short rounds**. Skip a question only if the user has **explicitly stated** the answer (in their request, attached files, or earlier in the conversation). Where you can make a good guess, **infer it and ask the user to confirm**: put your guess as the proposed answer ("I'm assuming this will be presented live by the panel, not sent to read. Right?"), so they can reply "yes" or correct it in a word. A guess is never a substitute for asking: a source file named "panel presentation" doesn't tell you this deck will be presented, and a request to review the look doesn't tell you its length. A wrong guess about use (read or presented) changes every headline, the text density, presenter elements, and animation, and is costly to undo. When you do skip a question, say which stated answer you're relying on ("you said it will be read, not presented, so…").

If the user says "just go" or won't be around to answer, take the most reasonable reading, state your assumptions at the top of the storyline, and continue.

## Round 1: essentials

**1. What should the audience think or do afterwards, and what's the one-sentence answer?**
- *Findings, e.g.:* "The steering group should approve option B. Our answer: a phased joint venture gives the most value for the least risk."
- *Workshop, e.g.:* "Program directors leave understanding strategic planning and signed up to take part in ours. Core message: it's a simple, repeatable set of questions, not a consultant exercise."

**2. Who's the audience?** (Position: for, neutral, or against? Interests: what do they know and care about? Style: detail or summary, analysis or recommendation? Expectations: update, decision, training, workshop?)
- *Findings, e.g.:* "Six executives across three government bodies. Broadly supportive but one is skeptical about cost. They want the recommendation, not the analysis."
- *Workshop, e.g.:* "About 15 program directors at a nonprofit. Mixed: some skeptical of 'consultant speak'. Practical people who want to know what this means for their day job."

**3. What kind of deck is it, and do you want it written or as a template?**
- **Type** (storytelling §5b): findings, recommendation, options appraisal, update/progress, proposal (pitching for work), workshop, or a mix.
- **Written or template:** a finished deck with your content, or a styled **template**: example slides of the kinds you'll need (executive summary, recommendation, timeline, chart with takeaways, options matrix…) with placeholder text that says what goes where, for you to fill in yourself (SKILL.md mode E).
- **Use:** presented live or read on its own; how long or how many slides; and, if presented, should reveals be animated? Default for animation: yes if it will be presented live, no if it will be sent as an attachment or read ahead (visual-identity §6).
- *Recommendation, e.g.:* "Recommendation deck, written in full, presented live in a 45-minute meeting, then circulated. About 15 slides plus appendix. Animate the build-ups; I'll need a static version to send afterwards."
- *Update, e.g.:* "Monthly steering-group update. Just give me a template in our brand that I can reuse each month."
- *Workshop, e.g.:* "Workshop, live, 80 minutes, with two group exercises. Animate the agenda and exercise steps. I'll want a share version afterwards."

**4. Brand and materials:** Is there a template or brand to match? What source material is there?
- *Findings, e.g.:* "Use the attached client template. Data is in the attached spreadsheet; interview notes are in the doc."
- *Workshop, e.g.:* "No template. Match the client's brand (their website is linked). My notes and the frameworks I want to teach are attached."

**5. Checkpoints: how hands-on do you want to be?** Offer the three options with their trade-offs, and recommend one (SKILL.md "Checkpoints"):
- **Review the storyline and the look before I build** *(recommended for anything important or high-stakes)*: two short reviews, each a minute or two. Costs one or two extra messages, but a wrong storyline or look is caught before an hour of building, and the deck needs far fewer revisions.
- **Review the storyline only**: I choose the look and build. Good when the story is what matters most and the brand is already set (a template, or a clear brand).
- **Build straight through**: fastest. I state my assumptions at the top and build the whole deck. Best for drafts, internal decks, or when you're away. Expect more changes afterwards, and if the story is off, a bigger rebuild.
- *e.g.:* "Show me the storyline and the look first." or "Just build it, I'll react to the draft."

## Round 2: details (ask only what Round 1 leaves open)

**6. Building blocks.** Show the section menu below, pre-ticked for the deck type, and ask the user to untick or add.

**7. Must-include or must-avoid?** (Data that has to appear, sensitivities, words the client dislikes.)
- *e.g.:* "Must show the cost waterfall. Don't name the other bidders. They say 'members', not 'customers'."

**8. Fonts on the presenting machine** (only if matching a brand that uses a non-standard font).
- *e.g.:* "Yes, the brand font is installed on my laptop." or "Not sure, so use a safe alternative."

**8b. Brand motif?** Is there a shape, symbol, or feature of the brand you'd like the slides to echo? (If not, I'll suggest two or three options from the logo or website and show you quick mock-ups to pick from.)
- *e.g.:* "The corner bracket in our logo." or "No idea, show me options."

**8c. Logos and photos?** Which logos should appear, and where (a co-branded lockup bottom-left on every slide, one logo top-right, or title and closing only)? Do you have photos to use? (If not, I'll build placeholder frames at the right size, labelled with the photo each one needs.)
- *e.g.:* "Their logo and ours, bottom-left on every slide. I'll send photos of their team." or "No photos yet, use placeholders."

**9. Navigation style and position?** How should slides show where the audience is: a short topic label above the headline (default), a tracker of the agenda or sections, a mini version of the framework with the current part highlighted, or a rail down the left? And where: top-left above the headline, top-centre, or bottom-centre above the page edge (with the notes above it)? Whatever is tracked must be introduced first (agenda, contents, or framework slide) and appear on every slide it covers.
- *e.g.:* "Topic label above the headline is fine." or "Track the agenda along the bottom centre, small." or "Use the six-question diagram as a mini tracker in the corner."

**8b. Corners?** Square or slightly rounded boxes? (Default: square, or slightly rounded if the brand has circular shapes. One style for the whole deck.)
- *e.g.:* "Square." or "Rounded, our logo is a circle."

**10. One version or options?** (For a key slide or the opening: one recommended design, or three structurally different alternatives to choose from.)
- *e.g.:* "One deck, but give me three options for the executive summary."
