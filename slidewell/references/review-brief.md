# Review brief

**Covers:** what the internal reviewer looks for in each review round (`qa.md` §2b), written as the questions a demanding reviewer asks of every slide, starting with the issues this skill's author raises most often.

**Doesn't cover:** the full rules (`finishing-standard.md`, `charts.md`); this brief points at them.

You are reviewing a deck someone else built. Your job is to find what's wrong, not to confirm it's fine. Look at each slide image at full size, one at a time. For every slide, either list issues or state that you checked it against every question below and found none. Then look at the slides in order to catch anything that jumps or drifts between them.

## The author's most frequent feedback (check these first, on every slide)

1. **Breathing room.** Is the content crowded under the headline? There should be at least ~0.6–0.8 in above and below the content group, with equal space above and below. When in doubt, the content should sit lower with more space around it.
2. **Header-to-content gap.** Is there clear space between each chart title or panel header and the chart or points beneath it?
3. **Same place, every slide.** Do chart titles, charts, panel headers, decision bars, and trackers sit in the same position as on the neighbouring slides of the same kind?
4. **Wrapping.** Any orphans (one word alone on a last line)? Any headline that breaks early, or leaves one or two words on line two? Any word broken mid-word? Any body line much shorter than the lines around it?
5. **Alignment.** Are parallel items exactly aligned? Are labels centred on the bars or shapes they label? Are side points level with the plotted data? Are markers between items exactly halfway?
6. **Colour roles.** Is the topic label distinct from the headline? Is each fill paired with the same text colour as elsewhere in the deck? Is the accent used only for the one thing to notice?
7. **Padding.** Is text pressed against the edge of its box (a price touching the bottom of its card, a label touching a border)?
8. **Collisions.** Does anything overlap anything else (labels over placeholders or bars, text over a photo without an overlay)?
9. **Accuracy.** Do the numbers add up? Does every duration label state its range? Does commentary match what the chart shows?
10. **Generic look.** Does this deck have its own identity: a palette and motif drawn from its subject, used in at least three roles? Or could it be any deck? Is any slide mostly a grey placeholder with little else?

## Then, for each slide

- **Story:** does the headline state one message, and does everything on the slide support it?
- **Hierarchy:** can you tell headline, labels, body, and notes apart at a glance?
- **Emphasis:** is only the primary layer bold? Is one item emphasised by grouping, not by making it bigger?
- **Charts:** title aligned with the axis labels, clear axis and units, labels inside bars centred, keys small and close or replaced by direct labels (`charts.md` §5b).
- **Finishing:** corners in one style, no shadows, small open arrowheads, page number low in its corner.

## How to report

A numbered list, worst first: slide number, what's wrong (specific: "price £56K touches the bottom edge of its card"), the rule it breaks, and severity: *must fix* (breaks a rule or looks wrong), *should fix* (noticeably weaker), or *polish*. End with one line on the deck as a whole: what would most improve it.
